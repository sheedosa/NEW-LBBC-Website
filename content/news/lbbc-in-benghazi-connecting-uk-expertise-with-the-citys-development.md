The Libyan British Business Council (LBBC) was in Benghazi this September for the Global Competitive Urban Planning Initiative (GCUPI), engaging with the organisations, specialists and international expertise contributing to the city’s future development.

GCUPI brought together expertise across urban planning, architecture, engineering, infrastructure and specialist consultancy, providing an important platform for discussion around the future of Benghazi. The LBBC’s participation reflects its continued engagement with the city and its role in connecting UK expertise with opportunities emerging across Libya’s wider development landscape.

A key part of the initiative was the opening of the GCUPI exhibition, which showcased the concepts and designs submitted through the programme. The exhibition offered participants an opportunity to explore different approaches to Benghazi’s future urban development and engage directly with the ideas and expertise being brought forward.

The LBBC’s programme in Benghazi also extended beyond GCUPI. On Friday 4 September, the Council attended the reopening of Benghazi University, an important milestone for one of the city’s leading educational institutions and a further indication of the investment and renewal taking place across Benghazi.

Together, these engagements provided the LBBC with a valuable opportunity to see developments firsthand, strengthen relationships with local stakeholders and better understand where UK companies and expertise can contribute across areas including infrastructure, urban development and professional services.

Building on the relationships established during the visit, the LBBC is now planning a business delegation to Benghazi later this year. The programme will give UK businesses the opportunity to visit the city, meet directly with key stakeholders and explore potential areas for commercial engagement.

Further details on the delegation will be announced in due course.
