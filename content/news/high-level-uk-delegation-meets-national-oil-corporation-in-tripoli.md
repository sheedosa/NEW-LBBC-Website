A high-level delegation of major UK companies from across the energy, trading, engineering and construction sectors met today with the Chairman of the National Oil Corporation (NOC) and senior NOC representatives in Tripoli, as UK–Libya commercial engagement in the energy sector continues to gather pace.

Organised by the Libyan British Business Council (LBBC), with the support of the British Embassy and through the LBBC’s partnership with the NOC, the visit follows directly from the Libya Energy Forum held in London in May 2026.

The delegation included senior representatives from BP, Shell, Vitol, Trafigura, Glencore, Metlen, KBR and Reid Steel, bringing together expertise spanning upstream development, energy trading, engineering, infrastructure and construction.

Meetings focused on practical areas for cooperation across oil and gas, power generation, infrastructure, solar energy and the wider energy transition, including specific projects and opportunities where UK expertise, technology and investment could support the NOC’s development priorities.

The Tripoli programme builds on the largest-ever NOC delegation to visit London, led by the NOC Chairman in May 2026. That visit brought senior Libyan energy leaders together with international companies and investors and marked an important step forward in UK–Libya energy engagement.

The return visit to Tripoli is designed to move those discussions forward, deepen the relationships established in London, and advance potential projects between UK companies and Libya’s energy sector.

Commenting on the visit, Peter Millett, Chairman of the LBBC, said:

"We had a great day with the NOC today. Our aim was to follow up on the large meeting held with the Chairman of the NOC and the visiting delegation to London in May, and to maintain the momentum behind the strong and growing partnership between the UK and Libya — particularly between the LBBC and the NOC. We brought a strong delegation of major UK companies, and we are determined to turn the warm welcome into positive business between the UK and Libya."

LBBC Director and Energy Specialist Haythem Rashed added:

"Global energy markets are under real strain, and Libya has both the resources and the partnerships to help ease it. The companies that joined us in Tripoli are among the most capable in the industry, and I am pleased that the discussions that have taken place during our visit involved concrete projects and opportunities in areas such as upstream development and innovative power generation solutions."

The visit reflects the LBBC’s continued focus on translating high-level engagement into practical business opportunities. Working closely with the NOC, the British Embassy and companies on both sides, the Council will continue supporting dialogue, introductions and commercial engagement that can contribute to Libya’s energy ambitions while strengthening the wider UK–Libya business relationship.

The delegation’s programme will continue in Tripoli with further meetings focused on identifying areas for partnership and progressing discussions with Libyan energy stakeholders.
