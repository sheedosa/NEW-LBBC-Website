Libya's decarbonisation agenda took a further step forward in London with LBBC member Capterio signing a Letter of Intent with the National Oil Corporation of Libya (NOC) to support the monitoring and reduction of gas flaring across Libya's oil and gas sector. The agreement was signed on 14 May 2026 at the Libya Energy Forum, following the "Gas, Flaring and Decarbonisation" session.

According to Capterio's announcement, the proposed collaboration will combine the NOC's decarbonisation ambitions with FlareIntel, Capterio's satellite-based flare monitoring platform, which provides near real-time data and analytics across Libya's oil and gas assets. The aim is to establish a nationwide flaring baseline, improve operational visibility, identify gas capture opportunities, and support the prioritisation of flare reduction and methane mitigation projects across NOC-operated assets and joint ventures.

The initiative aligns with Libya's wider objective of eliminating routine flaring by 2030 and reducing methane emissions across the sector. Capterio notes that capturing gas currently being flared could increase domestic gas availability, support exports through the Greenstream pipeline, strengthen energy security, and generate additional national revenues.

Under the proposed partnership, Capterio will provide continuous monitoring, analytics and opportunity identification through its FlareIntel platform, while the NOC will support engagement with operators and joint venture partners to help accelerate implementation. In its official statement, Capterio also thanked the LBBC and the British Embassy in Libya for their joint support in reaching the agreement.

The LBBC welcomes this development as a practical example of how UK-based expertise can contribute to Libya's evolving energy priorities. As Libya places greater emphasis on efficiency, emissions reduction and long-term sector development, partnerships of this kind demonstrate the value of informed cooperation between Libyan institutions and UK technology providers.
