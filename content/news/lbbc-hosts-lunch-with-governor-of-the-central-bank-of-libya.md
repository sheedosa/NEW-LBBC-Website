The Libyan British Business Council (LBBC) hosted a lunch in London with H.E. Naji Issa, Governor of the Central Bank of Libya (CBL), and members of his delegation, bringing together representatives from the Libyan and UK business communities for discussions on Libya’s economic outlook and the Central Bank’s ongoing reform programme.

The lunch was attended by representatives from De La Rue, NS Asset Management and the British Embassy, alongside LBBC sponsors BACB plc and Bank ABC. The meeting provided an opportunity for businesses and institutions with an interest in Libya’s financial and economic landscape to engage directly with the Governor and his delegation.

Discussions focused on the economic outlook for Libya and the reform agenda being pursued by the Central Bank. The meeting also considered the wider role of monetary and financial sector reforms in supporting economic development and creating an environment in which trade and investment between Libya and the UK can continue to develop.

The engagement comes at a time of continued focus on strengthening Libya’s financial and economic institutions. For UK businesses operating in or looking towards the Libyan market, understanding the direction of economic and financial sector reforms is an important part of assessing the wider business environment.

The meeting also provided an opportunity to strengthen connections between the CBL, UK businesses and organisations working with the Libyan market. Direct engagement of this kind allows businesses to raise questions, exchange views and gain a clearer understanding of developments within Libya’s financial sector.

LBBC Chairman Peter Millett, who hosted the lunch, said: “We look forward to building on this engagement and continuing to support the reform process in the months ahead.”

The lunch forms part of the LBBC’s wider programme of engagement with Libyan institutions, UK businesses and other stakeholders. Through continued dialogue in both London and Libya, the Council works to create opportunities for businesses to better understand the market and develop relationships with relevant institutions and partners.

The LBBC will continue to support engagement between its members and Libyan institutions as discussions around economic and financial sector reform develop.
